How validation works

Separating real findings from noise

A model grading another model's homework sounds like turtles all the way down. It is not, because the argument does not end in another model's opinion: it ends in something you can run against your product, and then a human decision. Here is one complete argument.

01

The claim

A researcher agent files a claim: invoice update skips the tenant ownership check. It attaches the source location and a cross-tenant request sequence. Starting confidence: 0.61.

Researcher
02

The challenge

A separate reviewer tries to reject it. First idea: the endpoint is admin-only. It checks the product and finds the role check runs after the ownership write, so the challenge fails.

Independent review
03

The rebuttal

The researcher does not just repeat the claim. It answers with the exact code path and a runnable proof of concept that reproduces the write as a normal tenant.

Researcher
04

The verdict

The claim is sustained and confidence rises to 0.94. If the challenge had held instead, the claim would have been revised or retracted, with the reason written down either way.

Evidence
05

The decision

A human records the lifecycle state and owns the risk. Rejected findings are not deleted: the verdicts, rebuttals, and reasons stay on file where anyone can audit them later.

Your decision

Why this is not turtles all the way down

The reviewer must reject, not grade

A grader can lazily agree with whatever it reads. This reviewer only passes a claim by failing to break it, so agreement is never the easy path.

Evidence, not another opinion

Challenges are answered with things that run: proofs of concept, HTTP sequences, deployable test packages against the actual product. Where the evidence runs, the argument stops.

A human closes the loop

The verdict arrives as a recommendation with the whole argument attached. People record the decision, and automation never silently accepts a risk on your behalf.

How you use it

You see the whole argument, and you decide

The argument runs on its own. Reading it, pushing on it, and deciding what happens next stay with your team.

STEP 01

Read the argument

Open a finding and see the full claim, challenge, and rebuttal trail instead of a bare alert with a score.

STEP 02

Push on it

Ask follow-up questions, request a different reproduction, or challenge an assumption over several rounds.

STEP 03

Decide

Record the lifecycle state, such as validated, disputed, accepted risk, or archived, with a written reason.

STEP 04

Revisit

Retests and regressions update the record, and past disputes stay auditable instead of silently deleted.

Outcomes

Less noise in the engineering backlog

Reviewed findings with visible evidence, and a written reason attached to every decision.

Less alert fatigue

Engineering sees reviewed claims with a visible evidence trail. Unsupported suggestions are filtered out earlier.

Defensible decisions

Disputes and accepted risks carry reasons that can be understood later.

Safer external sharing

Challenge high-impact findings before they reach customers, vendors, or bounty programs.
